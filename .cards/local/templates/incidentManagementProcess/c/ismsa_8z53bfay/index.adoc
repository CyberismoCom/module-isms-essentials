This document describes how the organisation manages security incidents in the scope of this home.

== Introduction

A practised incident management process keeps a security event from growing into a disaster that can stop the business. The process also makes sure that the organisation meets the reporting deadlines of the regulations that apply to it.

== Definitions

The NIS2 Directive, Article 6:

* (5) 'near miss' means an event that could have compromised the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of stored, transmitted or processed data or of the services offered by, or accessible via, network and information systems, but that was successfully prevented from materialising or that did not materialise;
* (6) 'incident' means an event compromising the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of stored, transmitted or processed data or of the services offered by, or accessible via, network and information systems;
* (8) 'incident handling' means any actions and procedures aiming to prevent, detect, analyse, and contain or to respond to and recover from an incident;

The NIS2 Directive, Article 23(3), says that an incident is significant if:

* (a) it has caused or is capable of causing severe operational disruption of the services or financial loss for the entity concerned;
* (b) it has affected or is capable of affecting other natural or legal persons by causing considerable material or non-material damage.

The Cyber Resilience Act, Article 14(5), says that an incident that has an impact on the security of a product with digital elements is severe if:

* (a) it negatively affects or is capable of negatively affecting the ability of a product with digital elements to protect the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of sensitive or important data or functions; or
* (b) it has led or is capable of leading to the introduction or execution of malicious code in a product with digital elements or in the network and information systems of a user of the product with digital elements.

The incident response lead records each incident in the incident register with the template "Cybersecurity incident". Near misses can be recorded with the template "Cybersecurity near miss". The incident card has two times:

* *Detected at*: when the organisation first became aware of the event.
* *Reportable at*: when the incident response lead decided that the incident must be reported. The reporting deadlines start from this time.

Until the incident card has fields for these times, record them in the description of the incident.

=== Step 2: Analyse the event

An event is an incident if it compromises the availability, authenticity, integrity, or confidentiality of data or of the services. An actively exploited vulnerability in a product of the organisation is also handled as an incident. So are problems with AI that compromise information or services, for example confidential information given to an AI tool that is not approved for it, wrong or harmful AI results that were used, and unexpected actions of an AI agent. A near miss is an event that could have led to an incident, but did not. Other security events are handled in the normal processes, for example in the management of vulnerabilities and updates.

The incident response lead assesses the impact with these criteria:

* Information impact: loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability, and personal data breach. Each is high, medium, low, or none.
* Functional impact: high, medium, low, or none.
* Expected financial impact: high, medium, or low.
* Impact on the security of the products with digital elements: high, medium, low, or none.
* Impact on other natural or legal persons: high, medium, low, or none.
* Recovery effort: regular, supplemented, extended, or not recoverable.

The assessment gives the severity of the incident:

[cols="1,2,3"]
|===
| Severity | Criteria | Actions

| High
| At least one impact is high, or the recovery effort is at least extended
a|
* The incident response lead sets up an incident management team.
* The security lead informs the management.

| Medium
| At least one impact is medium, or the recovery effort is supplemented
a|
* The incident response lead sets up an incident management team.

| Low
| All impacts are low or none, and the recovery effort is regular
a|
* The incident is handled in the normal operations. The incident response lead follows it until it is closed.
|===

Record the justification of the severity in the incident.

Then the incident response lead decides if the incident is reportable under the regulations in the table of step 3.2, and records the time of the decision.

=== Step 3.1: Respond to the incident

The incident management team coordinates the response. The response has these parts, as applicable:

* Containment: limit the damage.
* Eradication: remove the root cause.
* Recovery: return to normal operations.

If there are several incidents at the same time, the team prioritises the eradication and the recovery by severity. Complete the containment as soon as possible, so that the damage does not grow.

Record the completed steps in the task "Incident response" of the incident. If you suspect a crime, contact the police, and keep the chain of custody of the evidence.

The deadlines start when the organisation becomes aware of the reportable event: the time *Reportable at*. Check the national law for the exact deadlines and the receiving authority. The authority can also ask for intermediate reports.

The organisation can also report incidents that are not significant, and vulnerabilities that are not actively exploited, if the report helps others.

The external communications responsible plans the internal and the external communication. Use secured channels. If the normal channels are not available, use the backup channels of the business continuity plan. Only the named people give public statements.

Record the completed steps in the task "Incident communication" of the incident.

=== Step 4: Complete the post-incident actions

When the incident is resolved, store the evidence in the incident register, if possible, and set its retention time by the records retention plan. If the evidence is stored elsewhere, link it to the incident.

After high-severity and medium-severity incidents, the incident response lead organises a lessons learned session.

Record the completed steps in the task "Post-incident actions" of the incident.

== Maintenance

The organisation exercises the incident response at least once a year, and reviews this process in the exercise.
