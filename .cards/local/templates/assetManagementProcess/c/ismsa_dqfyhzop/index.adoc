This document describes how the organisation manages its assets in the scope of this home.

== Introduction

An asset is anything that has value to the organisation and therefore requires protection.
Assets can be divided into two categories:

* *Primary Assets*: Core business processes, functions and services;
Information and Data servicing business processes and/or activities of the organisation

* *Supporting Assets*: Hardware, devices and equipment, software and applications, roles,
locations and utilities, organisational infrastructure (e.g. policies, procedures and supporting ICT services)

== Process description

Assets are recorded in the asset register. If assets (with relevant details) for some categories are already recorded in some other tool, it is enough to add link for reference.


=== STEP 1: Identify primary assets, asset owners and locations (incl. backups)

. Core Business Processes, Functions and Services

. Critical Information/Data (including locations and backups) in all forms (storage, transmission etc) that is of value


=== STEP 2: Identify supporting assets, asset owners and locations

. Information systems

. Suppliers

. Information/Data (including locations and backups) in all forms (storage, transmission etc) that is of value

. Hardware, devices and equipment

. AI tools and services, and the AI features of other services. They are recorded in the register "AI tools" when the home has the AI use basics library

=== STEP 3: Classify assets

Each asset category has their own classification/valuation scheme. All assets are classified at least from the following perspectives:

* *Overall asset criticality*: High, Medium, Low

* *Criticality of confidentiality*: High (disclosure would have significant business impacts), Medium (disclosure would be undesirable but tolerable), Low (the asset is public, or disclosure would be harmless), Not Applicable

* *Criticality of integrity*: High (unauthorised changes have significant business impacts), Medium (some changes are tolerable), Low (integrity not relevant for decisions/business), Not Applicable

* *Criticality of availability*: High (days), Medium (weeks), Low (months), Not Applicable

* *Criticality for health, safety and environment (HSE)*: High, Medium, Low, Not Applicable

* *Risk evaluation needed*: Yes, No

Overall asset criticality summarises the four criticality dimensions above, and it should usually be at least as high as the highest of them.

Risk evaluation is mandatory for assets that are critical for operations (Overall asset criticality: High), but can also be conducted for non-critical assets, if justifiable (e.g. from brand-protection perspective).

[NOTE]
====
*Criticality of confidentiality is not the same thing as information classification.*

The *Classification of this card* field (public, internal, confidential) states how sensitive the asset *card* is, i.e. who is allowed to read this description of the asset. It says nothing about how sensitive the asset itself is.

How sensitive the *asset* is, is recorded in *Criticality of confidentiality*.
====

Additionally, information/Data and Information systems are classified at least against the following aspect:

* *Contains personal information*: Sensitive, Regular, None

Additional attributes can be specified in the asset register for each asset type as needed.

*Note*: If personal data is involved, related processing activities shall be documented into Records of Processing Activities (ROPA).

=== STEP 4: Protect and maintain assets

Asset owner is responsible for ensuring that risks related to their asset are identified as needed and their assets are sufficiently protected and maintained.

Risks related to most important assets are managed in accordance with the Risk Management process.

=== STEP 5: Asset disposal

When assets are no longer needed, they are disposed-off in a secure manner. More detailed device asset disposal principles are covered in IT documentation.

== Document/Process Maintenance and enforcement

The asset owners review the asset register at least once a year, in the review of the asset register.
